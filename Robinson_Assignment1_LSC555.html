The Filson Historical Society Digital Collections

System Introduction

Context

The Filson Historical Society, located in Louisville, Kentucky, was founded on May 15, 1884. It was named in honor if John Filson, Kentucky's first historian. The current building has been the home of The Filson since 1986.

System Specification

The Digital Collection consists of born-digital and digitized material. The online digital content is broken down into:

  • Digital Collections and Exhibits, featuring manuscripts, photographs, audiovisual, and museum items
  • Past Perfect Outline, which includes the photograph, print, and museum collections
  • First American West, includes collections of letters, financial documents, sermons, books, maps, and objects relating to the Ohio River Valley from the mid 1700s to early 1800s
  • Louisville Jewish Newspapers, which is digitized Jewish newspapers from Louisville, KY
  • Sustaining Slavery: Mapping Kentucky's Support for the Domestic Slave Trade, which is an interactive StoryMap that explores the trade of enslaved people in Downtown Louisville, KY

Users

This system was designed for those researching the history and culture of Kentucky and the Ohio River Valley, specifically those researching the historical American West, Jewish Newspapers, and slave trade in Louisville, Ky. Unfortunately, no usage data is available.

Content

The Filson Historical Society website states "These digital collections constitute only a small percentage of the more than 2.1 million manuscript pages and over 100,000 photographs in the collection."
The items in the digital collection include: title, subjects, description, creator, source, and date, when available.

Discussion on the System

Observations and Unique Features

  • The website does a good job at grouping collections into easily browsable sets.
  • The website is intuitive and traveling across the website is easy.
  • Allows for Boolean search, which I have never seen explicitly labeled before.
  • The interactive map is very interesting and adds to understanding of the slave trade in Louisville, Kentucky.

Pros & Cons of the System

Pros

  1. Navigation is easy and intuitive.
  2. Quality of digital scans is high
  3. High contrast text with a simple, clean layout makes it easy to read

Cons

  1. Digital Projects website loads very slow
  2. Hyperlinks are not consistent between descriptions
  3. Hyperlink in title of "Sustaining Slavery: Mapping Kentucky's Support for the Domestic Slave Trade" is broken
  4. Style of pages are not consistent throughout all of The Filson's Digital Collection websites
  5. Search function doesn't work with searches over one word, despite option for Boolean searches.

Suggestions for Improvement

I would suggest that The Filson Historical Society work to fix consistency across all of their projects. My first recommendation would focus on making the project description hyperlinks consistent in location across all projects. Second, a consistent style and layout across all of their websites would help with recognition. Last, I would recommend fixing the search function to allow for phrase searching to aid in usability for researchers.
